WHAT PICKING UP MEANS HERE
The cube has a free joint and begins on the table. Thumb/finger closure creates
opposing contacts. Frictional forces support it against gravity while the arm
raises it. Tests check height, contact continuity, slip, hold and final release.
Cube position is not animated or welded to the palm. Ground-truth object state
is used for scoring only; camera pixels/depth estimate the control target.
These results depend on the modeled friction, inertia and actuator limits.

WHAT IS LEARNED
42 contact-physics candidate trials produced six successful demonstration
grasp settings, one per training cube size. Ordinary least squares fits an
affine size-to-parameter policy. It predicts arm grasp offsets and finger
closure parameters. It is a real trained policy, but a small specialized one.
The raise/approach/descend/close/lift/hold/lower/release/retreat sequence is
explicit Python logic, and arm joint targets come from numerical IK.
It is not a general-purpose VLA, reinforcement-learning or foundation policy.

MEASURED RESULTS
10/10 held-out grasp tests: all complete pickup, hold and release.
6/6 camera-guided grasp tests: maximum initial estimate error 1.483 mm.
Grasp cancellation holds position; an empty target cannot pass the opposing-
contact gate to start lifting. The object is not artificially attached.
All 23 ROS arm checks pass, including real action feedback, cancellation,
RGB-D calibration/TF and ROS-triggered pickup with approximately 154 mm lift.
Seven navigation goals pass with no obstacle contacts. Pose RMSE is 8.0 mm in
the tested world. Replaying 1,217 sensor observations reproduces the map and
estimated path exactly. The occupancy grid comes from simulated noisy lidar.
Three combined docking and pickup runs pass. A physical wheel-driven base
parks using motor brakes; the mounted arm lifts the cube approximately 154 mm.
The combined experiment assumes an 80 kg chassis, clear docking lane, fixed
calibrated camera, known 36 mm cube and ideal base pose for manipulator IK.

SOFTWARE ROLES
FreeCAD/SOLIDWORKS existing files: the underlying human-shaped geometry.
MuJoCo: integration of masses, joint motion, bounded actuator effort, gravity,
collision/contact, wheel dynamics and rendered cameras/range sensors.
Python/OpenCV: calibration, orange-cube segmentation and 3-D backprojection.
Python/numerical IK: palm pose to seven arm angles; joint/velocity checking.
Supervised regression: size-conditioned grasp parameters in policy.json.
ROS 2 Jazzy: actual DDS JointState, trajectory actions, services, RGB/depth,
camera calibration, clock, TF, lidar, odometry and occupancy map interfaces.
Custom ICP and A*: scan-to-map local pose correction, map building, planning
with obstacle inflation, feedback driving and braking. This is not Nav2.
PyTorch/SmolVLM: actual local image-and-language inference. The cropped target
was identified as a cube. The full-scene description was wrong. Raw answers
and timings are preserved, and the VLM has no motor control authority.

NOT EXECUTED OR NOT CLAIMED
Jetson is a computing platform, not an additional physics simulator. There is
no Jetson device in this task. Its throughput, power and deployment are not
emulated by calling the Windows CPU a Jetson. Current components are tested
locally as requested. No physical sim-to-real transfer is claimed.
Isaac Sim/Lab were not installed/run because the available GTX 1660 Ti 6 GB has
no RT cores and does not meet NVIDIA's supported Isaac requirements. The
actual tested simulator is MuJoCo. See integration/runtime/isaac_feasibility.txt.
No general VLA, VSLAM, global SLAM loop closure, Nav2, MoveIt, arbitrary-object
grasping, or collision-free global arm planner is represented as complete.

SHARING
share/Simulation_Demo_2026-10-07.zip contains a portable offline presentation,
three recordings, posters and selected JSON evidence. Extract it completely
and open index.html. The recipient needs a browser for the recordings; they
do not need ROS or MuJoCo to watch. Running experiments requires the installed
project dependencies and the commands in each integration folder's README.

For a short spoken explanation: "This is a human-shaped simulated arm on a
wheeled platform. A virtual camera estimates the cube location, a trained
small policy chooses a grasp, and bounded motors drive the joints. Physical
contacts keep the free cube in the hand. The tests measure whether it lifts,
holds and releases; a separate noisy-lidar test measures mapping/navigation.
These recordings are software experiments, not proof of a manufactured robot."
