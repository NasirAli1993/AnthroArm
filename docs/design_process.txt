ANTHRO / ARM - HUMAN HAND DESIGN WALKTHROUGH
Project folder: D:\RoboticArm

Open http://127.0.0.1:8765/design while Robotics Studio is running.
The page presents saved construction views and simulation evidence in order.
Use Previous / Next, the left-hand step list, or the keyboard arrow keys.
Use Expand view to inspect a large image, and Compare original to put the
previous block-shaped model beside the currently selected stage.

1. SET THE PROPORTIONS
   A nominal 190 mm hand combines a 100 mm palm with a 90 mm middle finger.
   Segment lengths are joint-center design dimensions, not skin measurements.
   Index and ring: 40 / 24 / 18 mm.
   Middle: 45 / 25 / 20 mm. Little finger: 33 / 20 / 17 mm.
   Thumb: 30 / 20 / 15 mm, with two base axes for opposition and flexion.

2. SHAPE THE PALM
   Inspect the rounded palm outline, taper toward the wrist and raised thumb
   side. A palm shell gives the hand its silhouette and supports finger roots.
   The visible shell is distinct from the simplified collision representation.

3. BUILD THE FINGER SEGMENTS
   A proximal segment leaves the main knuckle; a middle segment connects the
   two smaller knuckles; a distal segment finishes at a rounded fingertip.
   The base has spread and curl axes, followed by middle and tip curl axes.
   Joint gaps must remain visible so the rigid shells can articulate.

4. MAKE THE THUMB OPPOSE THE FINGERS
   The thumb starts on the side of the palm. Its opposition axis turns the
   bending plane across the palm; flexion curls it toward the fingertips.
   It has a metacarpal, a proximal segment and a distal fingertip segment.

5. ASSEMBLE THE HAND
   Inspect the staggered knuckle line, differing finger lengths, curved shells
   and wrist transition together. Surface appearance is not a strength result.

6. TEST ARTICULATION
   The live simulator commands 20 finger axes and five arm axes.
   Choose a finger in Individual joint control, move one slider, and observe
   the reported angle and the rendered body. Open and fist exercise curl;
   pinch exercises thumb opposition and finger positioning.

7. MEASURE PRECISION
   Read http://127.0.0.1:8765/precision for the new model's measured results.
   A target angle is a command. The measured joint angle is the resulting
   position under gravity and contact. Their difference is tracking error.
   A screenshot alone cannot establish a numeric motion tolerance.
   Reported performance belongs to the tested simulation and conditions.
   The v2 report passes seven checks. Its 25 independent, contact-free joint
   tests settle for four seconds: maximum error is below 0.01 degree, and
   commanded palm-position error is below 0.01 mm (rounded for readability).
   This is an ideal model with known parameters and no sensor noise,
   backlash or structural compliance. It does not establish hardware accuracy.

8. TEST OBJECT CONTACT
   A seeded grasp begins with an object already placed in the palm, then
   closes and lifts. Its result applies to that setup. This is a different
   test from finding an object on a table and autonomously picking it up.
   The current recorded test lifted the 60 g block 293.2 mm, with middle and
   ring finger contact sustained through a two-second retention observation.
   Open http://127.0.0.1:8765/grasp-results for the complete measured record.

MOTION RECORDING
  http://127.0.0.1:8765/motion.webm opens the saved simulation video.
  Individual-joint control is available in the live simulation; the recording
  shows the saved run and cannot be used to command the robot.

UNDERSTANDING THE EVIDENCE
  Design dimensions: geometry parameters used to construct the model.
  CAD geometry: actual saved views of the model built in the CAD tool.
  Physics test: actual saved simulation views and associated measurement logs.
  Pending: a real image or result has not yet been saved for that step.
  An available image is not automatically a passed engineering test.

The walkthrough polls the saved manifest and image files while work proceeds.
It does not invent a completed stage when an image is missing. The explanatory
text describes the construction and what to inspect; the measured test report
is the authority for simulation results. The full CAD source remains editable.
