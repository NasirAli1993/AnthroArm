FROM THE CURRENT DIGITAL ARM TO A WORKING PHYSICAL PROTOTYPE
Evidence checked: 6 October 2026. Project files stay on D:/RoboticArm.

CURRENT CAPABILITY
The v3 camera demonstration is vision-guided approach and touch in MuJoCo.
It detects a known upright 50 mm orange cube in a virtual RGB-D image, estimates
its position, moves the arm towards it, and stops at contact. validation.json
and simulation/v3/vision_reach.py explicitly set autonomous_pickup=false.
Finger articulation and fingertip contact are separate checks. Neither proves
that an object is securely grasped, lifted, carried and released.
The SOLIDWORKS result was a fixed assembly. Its native finger sweep failed;
no native Motion/FEA pass or tested physical hardware is claimed.

HOW TO EXPLAIN IT TO AN AUDIENCE
"This is a human-shaped arm and hand concept with 27 simulated controllable
axes. The camera experiment currently demonstrates locating an object and
approaching it until contact. Secure grasping, lifting and physical hardware
operation still need development and testing."

WHAT WOULD QUALIFY AS PICKING
Proposed acceptance sequence, not completed results:
1. Detect and localize the object from a calibrated camera.
2. Choose opposing thumb/finger contacts and a reachable, clear approach.
3. Position the open hand; close selected fingers with bounded force.
4. Use contact/tactile feedback and joint feedback to verify the grasp.
5. Lift a known light test object clear of its support and hold it; verify
   separation from the table and absence of slip, rather than relying on
   the gripper-close command. Example first test: 20 mm clear for 5 seconds.
6. Transport a short distance, lower, release, and confirm release.
Record object mass, geometry, surface, repeated successes/failures, drop/slip,
force/current, motor temperature and cycle time. Simulation objects must remain
free bodies, carried by contact/friction rather than welded to the hand or
teleported along a prescribed path. Hardware results require physical tests.

PROPOSED PHYSICAL LAYOUT -- STILL REQUIRES DETAIL DESIGN
Shoulder / upper arm: load-bearing chassis, geared shoulder drives, joint
bearings and encoders. Existing motor shapes are packaging placeholders.
Elbow: geared output to a supported hinge shaft; bearings carry structural
loads independently of an inadequately rated motor output shaft. Brakes,
counterbalance or support may be required to control gravity loads.
Forearm: removable hand-drive tray, tendon spools, tension adjusters and
serviceable guides. Finger tendons run towards the wrist and palm.
Wrist: two driven joint axes and protected tendon/wire passages. Moving the
wrist changes cable/tendon geometry; pulley routing and compensation must be
engineered so wrist movement does not unintentionally close the fingers.
Palm: rigid finger-root mounts, thumb opposition mechanism, guides, sensor
connections and a small electronics board if actual clearance allows it.
Fingers: retained hinge pins/bushings, tendon guides, stops, tip anchors,
soft contact pads and optional tactile sensors. Selected large bench servos
are not intended to be installed inside each human-size finger.
Electronics: insulated standoffs on a removable tray; connector access,
strain relief, cooling and distance from moving tendons. No fitted PCB,
driver board or connector harness has yet been completed for this arm.
Bench/base initially: PC, power supply, stop controls and large controller.
After validation, compact controllers/drivers could move into the arm; all
electronics and batteries fitting inside is not established by this CAD.

ACTUAL FIT GAP IN THE CAD
The current forearm has 20 illustrative actuator envelopes, each
15 x 9.5 x 24 mm, and illustrative 3.5 mm-radius spools. No catalog motor
or reduction system is selected for these volumes.
The separate one-finger Hitec D645MW candidate is 40.8 x 20.0 x 37.8 mm
before horn/installation allowances. It does not fit those individual bays.
It is a bench-prototype candidate, not a selected full-hand motor.
The attractive shells cannot simply be printed and filled with the listed
bench hardware. Replace every placeholder with real manufacturer geometry,
including screws, horns, connectors, cable bends, bearings and access space.
Source-coordinate inspection also shows overlapping placeholders: the solid
shoulder/elbow motor envelopes share volume with the central structural tube,
and the inner forearm actuator columns overlap the two structural rails.
This is evidence of an unresolved arrangement, not a completed interference
study. Two large drive envelopes do not package all seven physical arm axes.
Separate shoulder-axis, forearm-roll and wrist transmissions remain unresolved.

HOW PARTS WOULD BE FASTENED
Build the inner chassis first. Bolt motor brackets to it and secure the
gearbox output to a supported joint shaft. Use retained pins, bearings or
bushings in the finger joints. Mount boards on insulating standoffs; clamp
wiring away from moving parts and leave controlled flex loops at joints.
Use tendon guides, rounded pulleys and adjustable terminations instead of
letting tendons rub on sharp shell edges. Shell panels attach with serviceable
screws and appropriately designed inserts/bosses after motion tests pass.
The current CAD fastener shapes do not establish thread engagement, bearing
fit, torque specification, manufacturing tolerance or load capacity.
Check the complete motion range with cables present, then inspect friction,
wear, voltage drop and temperatures under the intended duty cycle.

ONE-FINGER PROTOTYPE THAT CAN BE DEVELOPED FIRST
Use design/v3/printable_finger/TendonFinger_FitTest.FCStd or .step and its
stl_mm folder. This is a separate fit-test model, not the finished v3 hand.
Its nominal 40/24/18 mm links use 2.3 mm reference bores for M2 pins and
1.4 mm tendon-guide channels. Printed fit and pin retention must be checked.
Assemble and move the unpowered hinges by hand. Add a flexor tendon anchored
at the fingertip and a measured light elastic return. Mount the servo and
spool on a clamped bench base. Use a purchased compatible horn; check its
bolt pattern before finalizing the spool mount.
One servo pulls one tendon and couples three flexion hinges. This does not
provide independent knuckle/middle/tip control or lateral finger spread.
The 27-axis target has 7 arm and 20 hand coordinates. Reproducing all their
independent control requires a separately engineered drive/transmission
architecture. Motor count depends on that architecture; do not assume a
five-servo hand is equivalent to the simulated 20-axis hand.

ONE-FINGER ELECTRICAL CONNECTIONS ONLY
PC USB -> UNO R3 USB-B: board power and serial communication.
UNO D9 -> servo S: command pulses.
Separate regulated 6.0 V supply + -> appropriately selected fuse ->
DC-rated latching motor-power stop -> servo +.
Supply - -> servo - directly.
UNO GND -> common supply-/servo- ground reference.
Optional USB camera -> PC USB.
Servo positive does not connect to UNO 5V, VIN or an I/O pin.
Confirm actual connector S/+/- markings. Wire, fuse, connector and stop
ratings remain to be selected; do not route motor power through a breadboard.
This is a low-voltage bench arrangement, not the full-arm power design.
A servo has an internal controller; raw DC/BLDC motors require compatible
external drives and feedback. The full arm will need a selected power rail,
branch protection, regulators for logic, and rated distribution wiring.

COMMISSIONING AND ASSEMBLY ORDER
1. One finger: unpowered fit check; servo test with tendon disconnected;
   tiny motions after reattachment; record tendon travel, joint angles,
   current and temperature. Existing firmware starts disarmed and restricts
   command pulses to 1450..1550 us until measured calibration justifies changes.
   Its 750 ms watchdog detaches pulses; it does not remove motor power.
2. Instrumented opposing thumb and finger on a fixed base: develop actual
   contact detection, low-force gripping, holding and release tests.
3. Full fixed hand: validate each drive, thumb opposition, tendon routing,
   independent axes/coupling and harness. Keep covers removable.
4. Supported wrist and arm: select drives using measured downstream mass,
   center of mass, payload, acceleration, friction and transmission losses.
   Validate load retention, joint limits and cable behavior one axis at a time.
5. Integrate camera-guided approach with verified grasp control. Use calibrated
   camera-to-base coordinates. A normal webcam alone does not directly provide
   metric depth; use a calibrated geometric method or a depth sensor.
6. Fit covers only after assembly, clearance, thermal and service checks pass.

CONTROLLER AND SOFTWARE ROLES
CAD (FreeCAD / SOLIDWORKS): shape, fits, structure and assembly drawings.
MuJoCo + Python: simulated mechanics, joint controllers and contact experiments.
OpenCV: image processing for the current simulated orange target.
PC: vision, target selection, motion planning and supervisory commands.
Microcontroller: bounded actuator commands, sensor sampling and communications.
Motor driver/servo: electrical power conversion and low-level motor regulation.
Physical stop/power system: independent handling of motor power; software
DISARM is not an electrical disconnect or a complete machine safety system.
