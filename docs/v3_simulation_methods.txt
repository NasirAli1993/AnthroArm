ANTHROARM-27 - V3 SIMULATION AND CAMERA METHODS

293 native-frame CAD mesh solids form the complete shoulder, upper arm, elbow,
forearm, wrist and five-digit hand. Exterior surfaces use MuJoCo group 1;
internal hardware is group 4 and hidden normally; collision hulls are group 3.
Shadow casting is disabled in previews to avoid shadow-acne speckles on white shells.

The kinematic chain has 7 arm axes (shoulder X/Y/Z, elbow Y, forearm Z, wrist Y/X)
and 20 independent hand axes. Upper-arm, forearm and wrist pivot spacings are
300, 250 and 40 mm. Local +Z points toward the fingers, local +X is palmar.
The fixed shoulder is at world [0,0,1.08] m and rotated pi around X.
URDF retains this transform through its fixed world-to-shoulder anchor.

Each motion is integrated in MuJoCo at 2 ms. Position references are rate limited.
Model gravity/Coriolis bias goes through the SAME torque-limited actuators.
There is no body gravity cancellation or externally applied control torque.
All 27 free-space movement tests, hand gestures, bounded efforts, initial
clearance, three camera-guided touches and the missing-target rejection pass.

CAMERA AUTONOMY
The fixed virtual RGB-D camera renders real RGB and metric depth from the scene.
OpenCV segments the known orange target; calibrated pixel/depth backprojection
estimates its top surface and center. Known target dimensions are 50 mm cubed.
The detector assumes an upright target on the known 600 mm high worktable.
No object body position is read in the controller. Ground truth is read ONLY
in the validation program for scoring and initial placement of test objects.

The controller acquires an initial camera estimate, raises the arm for clearance,
aligns the palm above that estimate, and descends. Contact is checked every 2 ms;
physical hand/object contact stops motion. Upper-arm contact, missing target,
unreachable IK or worktable collision cause a stop without success credit.
The controller does not continuously chase a moving or occluded target.
A blue-target negative test yields no orange observation and no commanded motion.

This demonstrates autonomous camera-guided approach and touch, NOT autonomous
pickup. It is a controlled simulated RGB-D task, separate from the physical
webcam hand-gesture control. No physical motors are driven.

PINCH
The V3 fingertip preset is recalibrated against its inset-pad CAD geometry.
The actual contact occurs between distal flexor-shell convex hulls; those hulls
fill small pad pockets. This is valid fingertip contact, but not a simulated
isolated soft-pad deformation or exact concave collision calculation.

LIMITS
Mass/inertia, actuator capability, contact friction and rigid casing behavior
remain conceptual. Simulated tracking precision is not hardware accuracy.
IK does not provide a global collision-free planner. No learned grasp policy,
unknown-object recognition, SLAM, fabrication certification or real-motor test
is claimed. Physical manufacturing and commissioning require validation.

PHYSICAL CAMERA DIAGNOSIS
The available DirectShow source is ToDesk Camera (virtual). Windows enumerated
no physical Camera/Image device. DSHOW default, MJPG and YUY2 produced nearly
black/grayscale unusable frames; MSMF could not open camera index 0. Changing
the software backend did not supply a real feed. Live hand tracking has NOT
been verified. Enable a real camera feed through ToDesk or connect a physical
webcam before using hand gesture control. The final studio leaves camera off.
The diagnostic report contains scalar statistics only; no webcam image was
saved or transmitted externally. See results/v3/camera_diagnosis.json.
