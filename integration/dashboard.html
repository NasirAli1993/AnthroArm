The working pipeline

From geometry to an observed outcome

CAD supplies shape; physics supplies joints, forces and contact. Perception estimates a target, a controller requests movement, and tests measure the result.

Learning, precisely described

A small model with a narrow task

Six successful demonstration parameter sets train an affine regressor. Cube size conditions ten outputs, including palm offsets and finger/thumb closure. World position is handled by inverse kinematics.

Stage timing and approach/lift/release logic are programmed. This is not an end-to-end neural or reinforcement-learning controller.

Mapping, precisely described

Laser observations correct wheel drift

Encoder increments predict motion. ICP matches measured endpoints to prior measurements, correcting local pose. Ray updates build an 8 cm grid; A* plans with obstacle inflation.

Sensor-only replay reproduces the map and poses without simulator geometry or true-pose input. Initial position and orientation are known.

Five-minute presentation

Show the work in this order

  1. Play the grasp recording. Identify the cube, thumb contacts, lift and release.
  2. Play navigation. Watch unknown cells become measured space.
  3. Read a report. Distinguish success counts, pose error and goal distance.
  4. Run a live experiment and follow its status. Stop and reset when finished.
  5. Explain learned parameters, scripted stages and captioning limitations.
