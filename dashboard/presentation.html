Where OpenSCAD, URDF, cameras and other tools fit

OpenSCAD preserves a code-based geometry route. URDF describes links and joints for compatible robotics tools; a compatible importer and configuration are needed before claiming a ROS or Gazebo workflow. MuJoCo is the active physics engine here. A fixed bench hand does not need GPS or SLAM to move its fingers. Additional software should be introduced for a concrete experiment, with its own validation.

02 · Show motion and evidence

A demonstration should explain what it proves.

Use the live studio to move joints, then open the saved report for the current model. A target is a command; a measured joint angle is the resulting state after the controller and physics act.

Live demonstration sequence

  1. Open pose: identify the palm, thumb and three segments of each finger.
  2. Individual joint: move one selected axis and read the measured angle.
  3. Coordinated pose: compare an open hand, curl and thumb opposition.
  4. Contact view: explain why a contacted finger may not reach its target.
  5. Camera experiment: observe detection, localization and the commanded reach.

03 · Understand camera control

Imitation follows a person. Autonomy follows a task.

The camera itself does not choose a safe motion. Calibration, a state estimate, control limits and an outcome check must connect the image to the robot.

Webcam hand imitation

Camera → hand landmarks → mapped joint targets → simulation.

A person's hand supplies the desired pose. Tracking loss must hold or disarm according to the configured policy. A 2D webcam estimate does not provide reliable metric depth by itself.

Current computer: only the ToDesk virtual camera was found, and it returned distorted frames. Live hand imitation has not been validated with a usable camera feed.

Object-directed virtual RGB-D reach

Rendered RGB + depth → object location → coordinate transform → inverse kinematics → reach.

The v3 experiment detects a known upright 50 mm orange target with a calibrated virtual RGB-D camera. Three recorded trials approached and touched it, with position-estimation error below 1.5 mm in those trials. A blue-target negative test was rejected and held the joints. It is a specific simulation experiment, not general object recognition or autonomous pickup.

04 · Assemble the first physical stage

Start with one tendon-driven finger.

The printable bench finger uses three flexion hinges, one tendon and one servo. It tests routing, travel, current and return motion before the full hand or arm is engineered. Its coupled motion is different from the simulation's independent finger axes.

Candidate starter hardware

  • Arduino UNO R3: USB-powered controller; D9 is the signal output.
  • Hitec D645MW: one servo at a chosen 6.0 V operating point.
  • Separate motor supply: current-limited, fused and physically interruptible.
  • Printed mechanism: separate hinge, tendon, spool and mounting parts; inspect fit before assembly.

Hitec specifies 2.15 A stall current at 6 V. Stall torque is not a continuous rating. The motor is a candidate for the first measured bench test, not a selected full-arm drive.

Manufacturer servo specification ↗

Commission in small steps

  1. Check printed hinge fit and tendon travel by hand with power disconnected.
  2. Test the unloaded servo with the tendon detached.
  3. Verify polarity, physical motor stop and software disarm.
  4. Begin at 1500 μs inside the conservative 1450–1550 μs firmware window.
  5. Fit the tendon, measure motion/current and widen travel only after checking clearances.

The 750 ms watchdog detaches pulses. It does not cut servo power or guarantee that the motor releases.
