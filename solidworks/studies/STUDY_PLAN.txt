STEP transfers geometry; it does not automatically recreate the original
parametric design history, MuJoCo actuator model, joint limits, controllers,
sensors, tendon mechanics, or SOLIDWORKS mates. The arm contains conceptual
internal parts. Its simulation masses and motor-effort values are placeholders
for a digital model, not verified physical parts, material or motor ratings.

WHICH TOOL ANSWERS WHICH QUESTION?
Animation: demonstrates prescribed assembly motion and mate behavior. It does
not establish forces, structural strength, or an actuator's ability to move.
Basic Motion: approximate motion including mass, gravity and contact; useful for
visual review. Do not use its appearance as engineering sizing evidence.
Motion Analysis: use the Motion add-in for dynamics and result curves. Confirm
the available entitlement before promising this study type. Prescribed motors
force a motion; the resulting required torque must be compared with a measured
or manufacturer-supported motor envelope, rather than treated as motor proof.
Simulation static study: evaluates structural response under defined loads,
constraints and materials. Appropriate entitlement and a load-bearing design
are prerequisites. It is a separate analysis from assembly rigid-body motion.
SimulationXpress: a first-pass linear static tool for ONE solid body. It cannot
validate the multi-part hand/arm assembly. Validity depends on realistic loads,
fixtures and materials. Small, elastic response and slowly applied loads must
be reasonable; otherwise use an appropriate nonlinear/dynamic analysis.

00 - IMPORT AND MATE AUDIT
1. Open the STEP and confirm dimensions with Measure: upper-arm joint span
   300 mm, forearm joint span 250 mm, wrist span 40 mm. Do not assume the unit
   shown on-screen establishes correct imported scale.
2. Preserve separately moving parts. Rigidly group parts that belong to the
   same source link. Avoid accidentally fusing the entire arm into one body.
3. Fix only the shoulder attachment. Create reference axes from the joint table.
   All axes in joint_register.json are in the local joint frame, not world axes.
   The source +Z points toward the fingers; the shoulder anchor has a 180-degree
   X rotation in world. Retain the thumb's additional -0.75 rad X orientation.
4. Recreate each revolute joint using concentric/hinge and axial location mates;
   retain one rotation and add a limit-angle mate. Check sign with a +5-degree
   move before entering the complete range. Do not double-constrain the axis.
5. With joint-driving angle mates suppressed, confirm 27 intended rotations and
   no unintended translations. Prescribed motor studies drive these rotations;
   they do not add extra physical mechanisms.
Required evidence: imported-file screenshot, scale measurements, component to
link mapping, mate tree, rebuild report and signed-axis check for all 27 joints.
Pass: no import/rebuild errors; expected scale within 0.1 mm; all 27 rotations
accounted for. This is an import/mate check, not manufacturing tolerance approval.

01 - INDIVIDUAL JOINT MOTION
Use Animation for a first visual test; Motion Analysis only after masses,
inertias, friction and physical drive assumptions are documented.
For each of the 27 joints, keep other joints at source home, then move through
home -> conservative low -> home -> conservative high -> home in 8 seconds.
The JSON defines conservative low/high as home +/- 15% of that joint's source
range, clamped to its limits. Use smooth transitions and stop if it binds.
Then inspect the complete source range progressively, rather than assuming it
is physically feasible because a digital limit exists. Other finger positions
may need to change to provide clearance; record those configurations explicitly.
Export: one joint-angle curve and short video per joint; wrist and fingertip
paths; unresolved mates and solver warnings. If Motion Analysis is available,
also export required motor torque and joint reaction force, marked conceptual
until the mass/drive inputs have been verified.
Pass for kinematic import: driven angles within 0.5 degrees of the scheduled
values, continuous paths, correct sign, no unexplained motion/solver error.
No torque, speed or payload pass is permitted until actual ratings are supplied.

02 - INTERFERENCE AND CLEARANCE
At home, each conservative sweep endpoint and intermediate points spaced no
more than 5 degrees apart, run Tools > Evaluate > Interference Detection.
Also check pairwise shell clearance using Clearance Verification. Save component
names, intersection volume, minimum distance and the joint pose for each issue.
Repeat more finely near any suspected contact. Sampling does not prove continuous
clearance; use collision detection/contact or a finer sweep around moving edges.
Inspect shell-shell, shell-fastener, fingertip-palm and neighboring-finger pairs.
Review intended overlaps individually (for example, a fastener or reference
envelope). Keep an explicit exclusion list with reasons; never ignore all pairs.
Concept-stage target: no unexplained interference, and at least 0.5 mm nominal
clearance between non-contacting moving shells. The 0.5 mm value is a review
target, not a validated production clearance. Pin fits, bearing fits, pads and
intended contacts need their own requirements and tolerance stack.
Pass remains pending if a clearance requirement or expected-contact list is absent.

03 - SINGLE-FINGER LIMITED-ANGLE FIT TEST
Use the separate TendonFinger_FitTest assembly, NOT the full-arm visual fingers.
Fixed bench plate/root; three phalanx hinges about their M2 pin axes. Start with
the servo and routing illustrations hidden. The drawn straight cord segments
are reference geometry and do not implement a deformable tendon transmission.
First drive each hinge separately from 0 to 5, 10, then 15 degrees and return,
with all other hinges at zero. If a nominal start pose interferes, stop and
repair the assembly instead of forcing the motor through it. Sample each sweep
at 1 degree for this small-angle check. Record stop-tab behavior, pin/clevis
clearance and whether screw-head/retainer envelopes obstruct motion.
Nominal source dimensions: 2.0 mm reference pin, 2.3 mm bore, 6.0 mm clevis gap,
5.2 mm tongue, 1.4 mm tendon channel, 7.0 mm drum winding radius. These are CAD
dimensions, not measured printed fits. Do not equate a 1450-1550 microsecond
firmware window with a calibrated number of degrees on the actual servo.
Next, model the actual cord path, wrap/contact, stiffness, pretension, elastic
return and purchased servo/horn offsets. Only then try a coupled tendon study.
Begin with +/-5 degrees of drum rotation and review cord travel and joint motion;
this is a virtual commissioning range, not an authorization for a powered test.
Required outputs: hinge-angle plots, min-clearance table, tendon length/change,
drum torque and cord tension (latter two only with a defensible dynamics model).
Pass: intended limited-angle motion is free, no unintended contact, return path
is feasible, and the modeled transmission explains all three hinge motions.
Mechanical stop angles, motor fit and powered behavior remain unvalidated until
measured on physical hardware. One tendon/servo couples the three hinges; it
does not independently command three joints.

04 - STRUCTURAL LOAD CASES AND MOTOR SIZING (INPUTS REQUIRED)
Start with the finger root clevis, pin holes, drum/horn interface and bench
attachment. Full-arm shoulder/elbow loading comes after the physical structure
and drives are selected. Decorative shells are not assumed to carry arm loads.
Before solving, fill in:
- Material/process: polymer grade, print orientation, wall/infill settings,
  measured elastic properties/strength, metal pin/bolt grade and variability.
- Geometry: actual fillets, retained pins, bearings, inserts, fasteners,
  mount contact areas, shell thickness and tendon/horn interfaces.
- Loads: target fingertip force, payload and center of mass, tendon pretension,
  maximum cord force, full duty cycle, acceleration, gear efficiency and friction.
- Restraints: realistic bearing/contact support and fastener restraint; avoid
  fixing entire convenient faces if the real part is supported only at pins.
- Criteria: required safety factor, allowed fingertip deflection, fatigue life,
  temperature and acceptable permanent deformation; none is currently specified.
Useful calculations (do not insert invented load values):
  Drum torque [N m] = cord tension [N] * effective radius [m] / efficiency.
  Static joint moment = sum of (position from joint CROSS applied force).
  Required drive torque also includes acceleration, friction and transmission.
  Check pin shear, bore bearing stress, section bending and tendon-anchor load.
Compare peak, RMS and holding demands with rated drive behavior and thermal/duty
limits. Stall torque is not a continuous operating torque rating.
Use a static study only where linear/small-strain assumptions are appropriate;
elastic cords, soft pads, contact changes and flexible printed parts may require
different models. Do not apply an isotropic metal-like model to a printed part
and present the result as proven strength.
Refine the mesh around bores/fillets and repeat at least three mesh levels.
Proposed numerical review targets: reaction balance within 1%, meaningful
non-singular stress and displacement change below 5% on the last refinement.
Report mesh, units, loads, restraints, material source, warnings and convergence.
Stress singularities at ideal fixed edges must be diagnosed, not hidden.
Pass: numerical checks plus the hardware-specific safety/deflection criteria.
Those criteria and physical inputs are still missing: NO structural pass today.

05 - CAMERA/CONTROL BOUNDARY
SOLIDWORKS geometry or a Motion animation alone does not run the project's
OpenCV/MediaPipe/MuJoCo camera controller. Keep that working demonstration as a
separate, explicitly labeled result. Importing its joint trajectory could show
a replay in SOLIDWORKS, but replay is not live autonomous vision validation.
Future closed-loop coupling needs a documented API/co-simulation bridge, time
synchronization, collision policy and its own tests. No such bridge is claimed.
